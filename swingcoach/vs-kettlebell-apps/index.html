Approach by approach

When each kettlebell app is the right pick

1. SwingCoach (Camera pose & Apple Watch)

Verdict: Built for home and garage lifters who want hands-free counting and form feedback on swings, goblet squats, and deadlifts — or standalone Apple Watch counting.

  • Best for: Lifters building a clean hip hinge, running 10,000-Swing Challenges or EMOM blocks, and Watch users who want both wrist and camera modes.
  • Strengths: No ads and no account; readable rep count from across the room (portrait or iPad landscape); spoken cues in 26 languages; 4 movements; standalone Watch mode & 2.5 s motion assist; opt-in local video rep scrubbing; optional private iCloud Sync.
  • Trade-offs: Camera form estimates need 2–3 m side-view space and decent lighting (standalone Watch skips form analysis); 2D pose is a movement estimate, not medical advice.
  • Platforms: iPhone (iOS 17+), iPad (iPadOS 17+), and Apple Watch (watchOS 10+).

2. Pocket accelerometer apps (OmniSwing)

Verdict: Clever sensor-only counter that turns your phone into a swing pedometer inside your pocket.

  • Best for: Lifters training in dim rooms, narrow hallways, or busy gyms where setting up a camera across the floor is awkward, or anyone training on Android.
  • Strengths: Zero camera setup, works in low light, tracks swing cadence/RPM hands-free, available on iOS and Android.
  • Trade-offs: Cannot tell if you are squatting instead of hinging, bending your arms, or leaning back at lockout; loose athletic shorts can bounce and skew accelerometer readings; iOS background limits typically require keeping the screen awake.

3. Apple Watch wrist apps (BELLr)

Verdict: Friction-free wrist tracking when you already train with an Apple Watch and want power/tempo numbers straight into Activity rings.

  • Best for: Apple Watch owners doing pure 2-hand swings who refuse to prop up a phone and want upswing power analysis on their wrist.
  • Strengths: Instant start from your wrist, no camera framing or floor distance required, integrates directly with Apple Watch Activity rings.
  • Trade-offs: Requires an Apple Watch; many kettlebell lifters take watches off or flip them inward to avoid bell contact on cleans/snatches; wrist acceleration cannot diagnose hip hinge vs. squat form.

4. Interval & EMOM timers

Verdict: Still the simplest tool when your session mixes four or five different kettlebell lifts on a strict clock.

  • Best for: Mixed circuits (swings + goblet squats + presses + Turkish get-ups) or experienced lifters running fixed 10-reps-EMOM blocks who never lose track of short sets.
  • Strengths: Works for every exercise, zero calibration, runs in any lighting or crowded gym, pairs cleanly with music.
  • Trade-offs: Does not count a single rep and gives zero feedback when fatigue ruins your hinge on round eight.
Honest trade-offs

Where another tool beats SwingCoach

An app built around a side-view iPhone camera for Russian swings is not the right fit for every kettlebell workout.

  1. Crowded gyms or dark rooms: If you cannot place your iPhone 3–4 m to the side with your full body visible, a pocket accelerometer app (OmniSwing) or wrist app (BELLr) will save you setup frustration.
  2. Competitive Girevoy Sport multi-lift refereeing: If you train timed 10-minute competition sets of snatches, jerks, and long cycle with strict fixation rules, dedicated kettlebell sport camera apps (such as Kettlebell Swings by Diego Prados) target those competition lifts specifically.
  3. Mixed full-body kettlebell complexes: When a single round chains cleans, presses, squats, and carries, a simple EMOM or Tabata timer gets out of your way faster than a swing-specific coach.

Questions

Comparison FAQ

Is a camera kettlebell swing counter better than a pocket accelerometer or Apple Watch app?
Camera pose tracking like SwingCoach wins if you want both automatic rep counting and real form feedback (hip hinge, knee bend, arm length, and lockout) without keeping a phone in your pocket or wearing a watch. Pocket sensors (OmniSwing) and Apple Watch apps (BELLr) win in tight spaces, dim rooms, or crowded gyms where you cannot prop your iPhone 3–4 m to your side.
Do I need an Apple Watch to use SwingCoach?
No. Your iPhone or iPad camera counts reps and checks form on its own. With an Apple Watch (watchOS 10+), you can also train standalone on your wrist without a camera or pair with iPhone for Watch motion assist, Heart-Rate Guided Rest, wrist controls, heart rate, active calories, and Apple Health saving.
Does SwingCoach upload my workout video to the cloud?
No. Apple Vision pose analysis runs on your iPhone and SwingCoach never uploads camera frames or videos. Optional silent workout recording (introduced in 1.2.1, off by default) stays locally on your device unless you choose Share video. Read the SwingCoach privacy policy for details.
Which app should I pick for snatches, cleans, and Turkish get-ups?
SwingCoach counts and checks two-hand and one-arm Russian swings, goblet squats, and kettlebell deadlifts. For overhead presses, snatches, cleans, and Turkish get-ups, use an EMOM timer; for Girevoy Sport snatch/jerk competition scoring, use a dedicated kettlebell sport app.
