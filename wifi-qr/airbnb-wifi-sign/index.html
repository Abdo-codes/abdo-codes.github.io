What to put on it

What every Airbnb WiFi sign needs.

Keep the sign short. Guests read it from a table or a wall, often with a phone already in one hand.

Net

The network name

Write it exactly as the phone shows it. Network names are case-sensitive, so "BeachHouse" and "beachhouse" are two different names.

Key

The password

Use a clear monospace or distinct sans-serif font with generous spacing. In many fonts, O and 0, or l, I and 1, look identical. Every character must stay readable, even on a long password.

QR

A Wi-Fi QR code

Guests point the camera at the code and tap Join. There is no typing and no wrong letter. Keep the name and password in text too, for laptops, Apple TVs and game consoles.

Help

One line of help

"Open the camera and point it at the code." If guests may need to restart the router, add one short line noting where it sits.

Title

A welcome title

"Welcome to the Lake Cabin" makes the card part of the home and confirms immediately that guests are joining the right unit's network.

Host

How to get help

Add a short footer, for example "WiFi problems? Message us in the Airbnb app." Guests know the fastest way to reach you without calling.

Where to put it

Put the sign where guests look first.

Most guests look for the WiFi in the first ten minutes. Put one sign on their arrival path, and a second copy where they stream or work.

The entry table or kitchen counter This is the first flat place where guests drop bags, keys and phones right after unlocking the door.
The fridge, or a bedside frame A 4 × 6 or 5 × 7 card fits a small tabletop frame, an acrylic stand or a matte magnetic sleeve on the fridge.
Near the TV or the desk Guests sign in to streaming apps here, or work on a laptop that cannot scan a QR code and needs the printed text.
The welcome book and arrival guide Put a full A4 or US Letter page in your house manual, and mirror the same network name and password in your Airbnb/VRBO check-in instructions.
Size and printing

Pick a size, then print it to scan.

A WiFi card for Airbnb does not need to be huge. It needs a high-contrast QR code at least 3 cm (1.2 in) wide that any phone camera reads from arm's length.

4 × 6 in (10.2 × 15.2 cm) Photo-size card for a bedside table, fridge magnet sleeve, shelf or compact frame.
5 × 7 in (12.7 × 17.8 cm) Frame on the entry console, kitchen counter or wall. Easy to read from a couple of steps away.
A4 or US Letter Full page for the welcome binder, house manual, or a shared lobby or kitchen wall.
Print tips Print at 100% scale, not "fit to page". Keep the QR code at least 3 cm (1.2 in) wide, and use matte glass or a matte sleeve to avoid glare.
How guests scan

Guests join with the camera. No app needed.

A Wi-Fi QR code holds the network name, security type and password in a standard string. The phone reads them locally and connects. To share Wi-Fi straight from your own iPhone screen instead, read how to share Wi-Fi with a QR code on iPhone.

iPhone and iPad Open the built-in Camera app, point it at the QR code and tap the yellow Join banner.
Android Open the camera, Google Lens, or the QR icon inside Wi-Fi settings, point it at the code and tap Connect. See our iPhone to Android Wi-Fi guide for troubleshooting.
Laptops, TVs and game consoles These devices cannot scan a code. Guests type the network name and password from the sign, so keep both in large, high-contrast text.
Safety

Use a guest network, and keep the sign inside.

A WiFi sign shows the password to every guest. Set up the router for guest isolation and the sign is safe to leave out in every room.

Guest network Turn on your router's dedicated guest SSID so visitors get fast internet without access to smart locks, security cameras or your own devices.
Rotate when needed Change the guest password periodically (or after a long-term stay). The old static QR code stops working right away, so print a fresh card.
Strong password A long, random password is much safer. Because guests scan the QR code, password length adds security with zero check-in friction.
Inside only Keep the sign where only guests inside the unit can see it — never visible through a street-facing window or exterior glass door.

FAQ

Airbnb WiFi sign & card questions.

What should an Airbnb WiFi sign include?

Include the exact network name, the password in a clear font, a scannable Wi-Fi QR code at least 3 cm (1.2 in) wide, and one short line telling guests to scan with their phone camera. A rental welcome title and a short host-contact footer are optional.

Do guests need an app to scan a WiFi QR code?

No. The built-in iPhone Camera app and most Android cameras or Google Lens read Wi-Fi QR codes natively. Guests point the camera at the card and tap Join or Connect.

What size should an Airbnb WiFi card be?

A 4 × 6 in card fits a bedside frame or fridge magnet sleeve; 5 × 7 in fits an entry table or wall frame; A4 or US Letter works best for a welcome binder or house manual.

Can I print small WiFi cards for each room?

Yes. Wi-Fi QR includes a free Cut-out Cards template that prints 8 WiFi cards with dashed cut lines on one A4 or US Letter page. Place one card in each bedroom or hand one out at check-in.

How do I make a WiFi QR code for a vacation rental or VRBO?

Create a dedicated guest network on your router, then enter its network name, security type (usually WPA) and password into Wi-Fi QR on iPhone/iPad or our free local browser generator. Print at 100% scale and test with both iPhone and Android.

Can I make a printable Airbnb WiFi card in my browser without an app?

Yes. Use our free browser Wi-Fi QR generator to create, download (PNG or SVG) and print a WiFi card locally in your browser with zero signup and zero password upload.

Is it safe to put the WiFi password on a sign?

Yes, as long as you put guests on a separate guest Wi-Fi network (isolated from smart locks, cameras and personal devices) and keep the sign inside the home, away from street-facing windows.

Does the QR code still work after I change the password?

No. A static Wi-Fi QR code encodes the password directly, so it never expires on its own, but it stops working once you change your router password. Generate and print a fresh sign after each password rotation.

Is an app better than a Canva or Etsy WiFi sign template?

With a Canva or Etsy template, you generate the QR code separately and paste it into a graphic box, so editing the printed password often leaves an outdated QR code behind.
