Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Short version: Wi-Fi QR has no account and no ads. Networks you save stay on your device. The app sends anonymous usage events so it can be improved, and these events never include your Wi-Fi names, passwords or QR contents.

Usage Statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

Wi-Fi QR uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how the app is used. It sends:

  • App events, such as onboarding steps, the tab you open, a network created, edited, selected or deleted, a QR code created, the share or print sheet opened, the sign template and paper size you pick, whether you changed the sign text, a search opened, a scan started or cancelled, Settings actions, and errors when a QR code, sign or save fails.
  • App opens, sessions and time spent in the app, which Firebase records automatically.
  • A random app-instance ID that Firebase creates on install. It is not linked to your name, email or Apple Account, and it resets if you delete the app.
  • Device and app information, such as device model, iOS version, app version and language, and an approximate location (such as city, region and country) that Google derives from your IP address. Google Analytics does not store the IP address itself.

The app never sends Wi-Fi network names, passwords, QR code contents, scanned text or custom sign text. It does not collect the advertising identifier (IDFA), does not use the data for advertising, and does not track you across other companies' apps or websites. Google processes the data under its own terms: policies.google.com/privacy.

The app has no account system, no advertising SDK and no developer-operated server.

Network Information Stored on Your Device

When you save a network in Wi-Fi QR, the app stores the network details needed to regenerate the QR code on your device. This can include:

  • Network name (SSID)
  • Password, if the network requires one
  • Security type
  • Hidden-network setting

This information is local to your device and is not sent to the developer.

Location Permission

iOS requires location permission before an app can read the current Wi-Fi network name. Wi-Fi QR uses this permission only to fill in the current network name for you.

Wi-Fi QR does not store or share your precise location. The approximate region in usage statistics comes from your IP address, not from this permission.

QR Code Sharing

Generated QR codes can contain the Wi-Fi details needed to join the network. QR images leave the app only when you choose to share, save, print, or send them through iOS sharing features.

Anyone who can scan a QR code containing your Wi-Fi password may be able to join that network, so share QR codes only with people you trust.

Deleting Your Data

You can remove saved network information by deleting saved networks in the app where available, or by deleting Wi-Fi QR from your device. Deleting the app removes its local app data from iOS and resets the app-instance ID, so new usage statistics are not linked to the old ones.

Children's Privacy

Wi-Fi QR is a general utility and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to This Policy

This policy may be updated if Wi-Fi QR changes how it handles data. The updated policy will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
