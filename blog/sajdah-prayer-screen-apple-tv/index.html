The Prayer Screen: prayer times that look good on your TV all day

Most TVs sit dark between shows. With Sajdah, the biggest screen at home can show the day's prayers instead, in a design that fits the room.

Abdelrahman Eita · 27 October 2026 · Updated 24 November 2026 · 6 min read

In many homes, the prayer times live on a phone. You unlock it, open an app, and look for the next prayer. In a masjid it is different: the times hang on the wall, and everyone in the room can see them. I wanted that feeling at home, on the screen the whole family already looks at.

That is the Prayer Screen in Sajdah, the Quran and Athan app for Apple TV. It shows the day's prayer times full screen, with a live countdown to the next prayer, and it stays on between prayers. This post walks through all eleven styles, the Liquid Glass Quick Control Dock, and the small decisions that make it work on a TV.

In short

  • Eleven full-screen styles: Classic, Focus, Horizon, Timetable, Rosette, Board, Masjid, Sanctuary, Mihrab, Chronograph and Ayat
  • Auto-hiding Liquid Glass Quick Control Dock: switch styles directly, play background Quran audio, toggle 12h/24h clock and show or hide the daily verse
  • Made to read from the sofa: one large name, one large countdown
  • Arabic names and full Arabic layout
  • Safe to leave on all day, with burn-in protection
  • Living-sky Horizon free for everyone, and all eleven styles with Sajdah Premium

How it works

Open the Prayer Screen from its tile on the Today screen. The screen fills with the day's times and stays clean while it runs:

  • Swipe up or down on the remote to step through styles.
  • Press Select on any unlocked style to open the Quick Control Dock.
  • Press Back to leave.

Sajdah remembers the last style you picked, along with your preferred clock format and verse setting, so the next time it opens the way you left it.

The Liquid Glass Quick Control Dock

When you want to change something without leaving the Prayer Screen, press Select on the Siri Remote. A translucent Liquid Glass dock rises from the bottom and hides itself again as soon as you finish:

  • Visual style picker: scroll across all eleven styles as thumbnail cards and jump straight to the one you want.
  • Play Quran / Pause Audio: start or pause background Quran or radio audio right from the Prayer Screen.
  • Clock format: cycle between Auto (matches your Apple TV locale), 12h and 24h, saved across sessions.
  • Verse strip: toggle the devotional verse strip on or off, saved automatically.

Eleven styles for every living room

Every style shows the same facts: the next prayer, a live countdown to it, all six times of the day (Fajr, Sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha), and the Hijri and Gregorian dates. What changes is the mood and density of the room.

Classic

Dark green, with the name of the next prayer in a large serif and a gold countdown under it. The six times sit in one row at the bottom, and the next one has a frame around it. This is the calm, simple choice.

Focus

Deep navy, with a thin gold ring around the next prayer. The ring fills as the time between two prayers passes, so you can see how close the next one is without reading a number.

Horizon (Free)

The path of the sun across the day, from Fajr to Isha. Each prayer sits at its place on the arc, a glowing dot marks the current time, and the living sky behind the arc shifts with the time of day — cool dawn teal at Fajr and morning, warm plum and amber at Maghrib dusk, and deep indigo at night. It is free for everyone and opens first.

Timetable

A clear table, like the board at the door of a masjid. The left side shows the next prayer in large letters with its countdown. The right side lists each prayer in English and Arabic with its time, and a bar shows how much of the wait has passed.

Rosette

The day as a dial. The six prayers sit around a circle, and a violet arc runs from the last prayer to the next. The Arabic name of the next prayer, الظهر in this picture, sits at the center.

Board

Black, like a departure board at an airport. Each prayer has a numbered row with its status: passed, next with a countdown, or the time left until it comes. The clock sits at the top corner. This is the one guests ask about.

Masjid

Built for families who love the clarity of a real mosque wall board. A crisp digital clock with ticking amber seconds anchors the top left, the next prayer card shows both the countdown and the Iqamah pill, and the schedule on the right puts Athan and Iqamah side by side in emerald and gold.

Sanctuary

Soft, layered twilight hills and an ultra-light modernist clock give Sanctuary the feel of a quiet gallery screen. The next prayer rests in a compact corner card, and the six daily times float together in a clean glass bar along the bottom.

Mihrab

A delicate gold architectural mihrab arch with a hanging diamond lantern frames the Arabic calligraphy of the next prayer and its live countdown, flanked by subtle geometric stars on an obsidian backdrop.

Chronograph

The quietest option for dark rooms and OLED TVs at night. Huge ultra-thin digits sit over a near-black canvas with a warm whisper halo, a single amber pill carries the next prayer and countdown right beneath the clock, and all six times stay muted along the lower edge.

Ayat

Turns the TV into a quiet daily reflection. The Quran Verse of the Day sits at the center of a deep emerald canvas in crisp Arabic calligraphy with its translation and surah citation, while the live clock, next-prayer pill and six daily times stay readable around the perimeter.

Made to read from the sofa

A TV is three meters away, not thirty centimeters. So each style puts one thing first: the name of the next prayer and how long until it comes. You can read both from across the room in a second. The other times are smaller, and the prayers that have passed are dimmer, so your eye goes to what matters now.

The countdown and times use numbers of equal width, so the digits do not jump as the seconds change. The colors are dark and soft, so the screen does not light up the room at night.

In Arabic, too

When your Apple TV is in Arabic, all eleven Prayer Screen styles turn to Arabic. The layout reads from right to left, the names and dates are in Arabic, and the numbers use Arabic digits. Timetable, Masjid and Board show the Arabic name next to the English one in both languages, so a family that speaks both can read it.

Safe to leave on all day

A screen that stays on needs care, so the Prayer Screen does three things for you:

  • It keeps the TV awake. While it is open, the Apple TV does not go to its screen saver or to sleep.
  • It moves slowly. Every two minutes, the content moves a few points along a small oval. You will not see it, but no pixel holds the same image for hours, which helps protect OLED TVs from burn-in.
  • It dims when no one uses it. After ten minutes with no remote press, the screen goes down to 70% brightness. Press any button and it comes back to full.

Free Horizon and Sajdah Premium

Horizon — complete with its dynamic living sky and the Quick Control Dock for Quran audio, 12h/24h clock format and verse visibility — is free for everyone, using the real prayer times for your city. Sajdah Premium unlocks the other ten styles (Classic, Focus, Timetable, Rosette, Board, Masjid, Sanctuary, Mihrab, Chronograph and Ayat) along with Iqamah countdowns.

Without Premium, you can still swipe to preview every locked style live with your own city's times. A small chip at the bottom notes that the style is part of Premium; press Select to see the Premium options, or leave the remote alone and Horizon comes back after 30 seconds.

Premium also adds the Athan in 13 voices at every prayer, Qibla direction, and 15 calculation methods so the screen and the call to prayer work together. Frequently asked questions

Is Sajdah's Prayer Screen safe to leave on an OLED TV all day? Yes. The Prayer Screen uses dark, soft palettes, shifts pixels slightly along a subtle oval every two minutes to prevent static burn-in, and dims to 70% brightness after ten minutes without remote input.

Can I use the Prayer Screen for free? Yes. Dynamic living-sky Horizon is free for everyone using your real local prayer times, including the Quick Control Dock for background Quran audio, 12h/24h clock format and verse visibility. You can also swipe to preview the other ten styles before choosing monthly, yearly or one-time Lifetime Premium.

Does the Prayer Screen work in Arabic and match my local mosque times? Yes. When Apple TV is set to Arabic, all eleven Prayer Screen styles switch to right-to-left Arabic with Arabic digits. With Sajdah Premium you can pick any city, one of 15 calculation methods (Standard or Hanafi Asr), a short-night rule above 48° latitude, Iqamah countdowns, and adjust each prayer by up to 30 minutes.

Can I play Quran audio and hear the Athan while the Prayer Screen is open? Yes. Press Select on any unlocked style to open the Quick Control Dock and play or pause background Quran recitation directly from the Prayer Screen. Sajdah Premium also pairs the Prayer Screen with 13 Athan voices (plus a dedicated Fajr Athan) so the call to prayer sounds automatically from your living-room TV.
