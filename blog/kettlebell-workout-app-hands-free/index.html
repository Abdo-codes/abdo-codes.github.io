Release notes · SwingCoach 1.2 for iPhone

Kettlebell workout app: hands-free, with a huge rep count

SwingCoach is a free kettlebell workout app for iPhone and Apple Watch. Version 1.2 has a dark look with one number on each workout screen, and it ends your workout when you stop.

Abdelrahman Eita · 15 November 2026 · 4 min read

A kettlebell workout app must work when your phone is on the floor and your hands are busy. SwingCoach kettlebell workout app for iPhone does this. Your iPhone camera counts each swing, and the app speaks short cues. SwingCoach 1.2, out on 13 November 2026, makes the app easier to read and easier to end.

In short

  • A new dark look with one orange accent and one big number.
  • Hands-free end: stand still for 5 seconds and the End dialog opens.
  • The coach says "Session saved." when your result is saved.
  • Round workouts show the rounds you reached, and Skip rest starts the next round.
  • The workouts in SwingCoach, with a simple plan for home.

A new look you can read from across the room

The app is now dark, with one orange accent. Each workout screen shows one large number. Put the phone 3 to 4 m away and you can still read it. This is the main job of a hands-free workout app: you look, you see the count, and you keep swinging.

The kettlebell dial on the Train screen also reacts when you tilt or shake your phone.

A hands-free end

You do not need to walk to the phone when you finish. Stand still for 5 seconds after your last swing, and the End dialog opens. The coach then says "Session saved." when your result is saved. You can keep your hands on the bell and your eyes on the room.

Better round workouts

  • The result of a round workout shows the rounds you reached.
  • Skip rest starts the next round now. It works as a simple kettlebell timer: you do not wait if you feel ready.
  • The camera check is clearer, and every workout type has a way back from it.

The workouts in SwingCoach

  • Rep goal. Pick 10, 20, 30 or 50 swings, or set your own count. The workout ends when you reach it.
  • Open training. There is no goal. You swing at your own pace and finish when you are ready.
  • Starter program. A plan of three sessions. You do rounds of a few swings, with rest between them. The last session repeats the camera check.
  • Swing ladder. 29 rounds of one minute. You do 1 swing, then 2, up to 15, then count back down to 1.

You can pause, resume or end any workout. At the start, SwingCoach asks for a five-swing check. It gives you one thing to focus on, then builds your plan.

A simple kettlebell workout at home

You need one kettlebell, an iPhone and a clear floor. This is how a first week can look with the app.

  1. Put the iPhone at hip height, 3 to 4 m away. Stand sideways to the camera.
  2. Do the five-swing check. The app counts your swings and tells you one place to focus.
  3. Start Session 1 of the starter program. It is a beginner kettlebell plan, and the first session is called "Build the hinge".
  4. Use Skip rest if you feel ready for the next round.
  5. Stand still for 5 seconds at the end. Wait for "Session saved."

SwingCoach gives movement estimates from a 2D side view. It is not a medical assessment. Train within your ability and clear the space around you before you swing.

Apple Watch

The Apple Watch app is optional. It shows your heart rate and active calories, and you can start, pause and end a workout from your wrist. The iPhone camera still does the counting. The SwingCoach 1.1 release notes describe the Watch screens, and our kettlebell swing counter comparison breaks down camera vs. Watch vs. pocket apps.

Where SwingCoach started

I built SwingCoach for the Russian swing. The launch post explains why I made a phone app and why it counts only the swing. Version 1.1 added the hands-free start and the spoken cues, as the 1.1 post describes.
