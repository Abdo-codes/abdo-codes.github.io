How to share your Wi-Fi password with a QR code on iPhone

Show a built-in Wi-Fi QR code in four taps on iOS 18, create a Personal Hotspot QR code, or print a guest card that works for both iPhone and Android.

Abdelrahman Eita · Updated 23 November 2026 · 6 min read
Read in: Español Deutsch
TL;DR · Quick answer

To show a Wi-Fi QR code on iPhone (iOS 18 or later): open the Passwords app → tap Wi-Fi → tap your network → tap Show Network QR Code. Anyone with an iPhone or Android camera can scan your screen to join immediately.

  • 1. Passwords app (iOS 18+): fastest for one guest next to you on a network you already joined.
  • 2. Share Password: one-tap prompt for nearby Apple devices in your Contacts (no QR code).
  • 3. Printable QR card or saved widget: works on iOS 17+, hidden/WEP networks, rentals and Personal Hotspots.

I built Wi-Fi QR because iPhone used to have no built-in way to share a Wi-Fi password with Android guests or print a guest card. Since iOS 18, Apple added a QR code right inside the Passwords app — so now you have three strong options depending on who is visiting.

1. Show a Wi-Fi QR code from the Passwords app (iOS 18+)

In iOS 18 and later, Apple's built-in Passwords app stores every Wi-Fi network your iPhone has joined and can render a scannable QR code on the spot.

  1. Open the Passwords app and unlock it with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.
  2. Tap the Wi-Fi tile.
  3. Tap the network you want to share.
  4. Tap Show Network QR Code and hold up your screen for your guest to scan.

This is the quickest choice when one person is standing beside you, and it works for both iPhone and Android cameras. It does have three practical limits:

  • It only shows networks this iPhone already joined (not a separate router, guest VLAN you never connected to, or your own Personal Hotspot).
  • You have to unlock Passwords and hand over or hold up your phone each time someone asks.
  • It cannot export or print a clean guest card for a bedroom, desk or vacation rental.

2. Share Password with a nearby Apple device (no QR code)

If the other person has an iPhone, iPad or Mac, iOS can beam the password over Bluetooth without showing a QR code.

  1. Unlock your iPhone and make sure it is connected to the Wi-Fi network.
  2. On the other Apple device, open Settings → Wi-Fi and tap the same network name.
  3. On your iPhone, tap Share Password when the sheet pops up, then tap Done.

Both devices need Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on, must be within a few feet, and each person must have the other's Apple Account email saved in Contacts. It never works for Android phones, Windows laptops, or guests who arrive while you are out.

3. Make a QR code you can save as a widget, send or print

If you want a QR code you never have to dig for — pinned in a Photos Favorites album, placed in a Home Screen Photos widget, sent in a check-in text, or printed on a bedside card — create a static QR code once with Wi-Fi QR (free on iPhone and iPad, iOS 17+) or our free local browser generator.

  1. Install Wi-Fi QR (free, no account, no ads).
  2. Add a network by typing the name and password, or tap Scan Router Sticker to read the label with your camera.
  3. Pick the security type (WPA/WPA2/WPA3, WEP, or Open) and flip on Hidden Network if the SSID is hidden.
  4. Tap Share QR Code to save the image to Photos (great for a Home Screen or Lock Screen quick album), or tap Print Guest Sign to print a 4 × 6, 5 × 7, A4, US Letter card or 8 cut-out room cards.

Your network details stay strictly on your device. You can also try the free browser Wi-Fi QR generator right now without installing an app, or read our Airbnb WiFi sign & printable card guide for templates, exact sizes and placement tips.

How to create an iPhone Personal Hotspot QR code

Apple's Passwords app does not generate a QR code for your own iPhone Personal Hotspot, because your hotspot is a network your phone broadcasts rather than one it joins. You can still make a permanent, scannable Hotspot QR code in 30 seconds:

  1. Open Settings → Personal Hotspot and switch on Allow Others to Join.
  2. Note your Wi-Fi Password here, and verify your exact hotspot name under Settings → General → About → Name (including spaces or apostrophes).
  3. Open Wi-Fi QR (or the free browser generator), enter that exact name and password, and keep Security set to WPA.
  4. Save the QR image to your iPhone Photos Favorites album. Whenever a travel companion or Android user needs your hotspot, toggle Personal Hotspot on and flash the saved QR image.

Tip: Keep the Personal Hotspot screen open for a moment while the other phone scans so iOS actively advertises the hotspot SSID. If an older laptop or Android phone struggles to see the hotspot, toggle on Maximize Compatibility on the same Settings screen.

How to connect an iPhone or Android using a Wi-Fi QR code

  • iPhone and iPad: open the built-in Camera app, point it steadily at the QR code, tap the yellow Join Network banner, and tap Join.
  • Android: open the default Camera app, Google Lens, or tap the small QR icon inside Settings → Wi-Fi, then tap Connect. See the full iPhone to Android Wi-Fi sharing guide for Android model specifics.
  • Laptops and TVs: these cannot scan QR codes. Always print or display the network name and password next to the code.

What is inside a Wi-Fi QR code

A standard Wi-Fi QR code is just one line of plain text. For a typical WPA2/WPA3 home router, it looks like this:

WIFI:T:WPA;S:Lake Cabin;P:the-password;;

  • T is the security type: WPA (covers WPA, WPA2 and WPA3 Personal), WEP for legacy routers, or nopass for open networks.
  • S is the case-sensitive network name (SSID), and P is the password.
  • H:true flags a hidden network.

If your network name or password contains \, ;, ,, : or ", it must be escaped with a leading backslash. Skipping escaping creates a QR code that cameras scan, but phones fail to authenticate. I wrote more about these edge cases in the story behind Wi-Fi QR.

When the Wi-Fi QR code does not work

  • The iPhone camera shows no Join banner: open Settings → Camera and verify Scan QR Codes is switched on. Move slightly back, raise screen brightness, and avoid overhead glare.
  • The phone reads the code but fails to join: double-check capitalization in both the SSID and password, and verify the security type matches your router.
  • It worked before, not anymore: the router password changed. Static QR codes encode the password directly, so generate a fresh code.
  • Hidden network will not connect: make sure H:true (Hidden Network toggle) is turned on when creating the code.

Frequently asked questions

How do I show a Wi-Fi QR code on my iPhone?
On iOS 18 or later, open the Passwords app, tap Wi-Fi, tap your network, and tap Show Network QR Code. Your iPhone displays a scannable QR code that both iPhone and Android cameras can read.
How do I connect an iPhone using a Wi-Fi QR code?
Open the built-in iPhone Camera app (no extra app needed), point it at the Wi-Fi QR code, and tap the yellow Join Network banner at the bottom of the viewfinder, then tap Join. Make sure Scan QR Codes is enabled in Settings → Camera.
How do I create a Personal Hotspot QR code on iPhone?
Apple's Passwords app does not generate a QR code for your own Personal Hotspot. Open Settings → Personal Hotspot, turn on Allow Others to Join, note your iPhone's hotspot name (Settings → General → About → Name) and Wi-Fi Password, and enter them into Wi-Fi QR or our local browser generator with WPA security. Save the QR image to Photos for one-tap sharing.
Can an Android phone scan a Wi-Fi QR code from an iPhone?
Yes. Wi-Fi QR codes use a universal standard format. Point the Android camera, Google Lens, or the QR scanner icon inside Android Wi-Fi settings at the iPhone's QR code and tap Connect or Join.
Why is "Show Network QR Code" missing on my iPhone?
Show Network QR Code requires iOS 18 or later and only appears for Wi-Fi networks that the iPhone has already joined. On iOS 17 or earlier, or for a router you have not joined yet (including WEP or hidden networks), use Wi-Fi QR or our free browser generator.
Can I print my iPhone Wi-Fi QR code as a card for guests?
The Passwords app only shows the code on screen. To print a 4 × 6, 5 × 7, A4, US Letter sign or 8 cut-out room cards, use the free Wi-Fi QR app on iPhone or our free local browser generator.
